# v10: live analytics, critical emergencies, Showdown battles and first person

Implemented directly in `C:\Pokemon Day - Among Us` on 2026-10-26. The user's clarified rule is authoritative: only injected emergencies/scenarios have the fatal one-minute deadline. Pokemon encounters retain their independent timer.

## What changed and why

1. **Player predictions:** the live adviser previously queued repeated full reducer continuations and competing plan comparisons; a five-minute round could outlast its advice. Live analysis now calls a direct authoritative endpoint and compares four route/responder schedules with 64 matched timing samples each, avoiding the worker queue. Canonical travel, remaining puzzle work, paired controls, active incident deadlines and both extraction arrivals drive the forecast. Material changes discard stale results immediately, factual next steps display immediately, stable numeric refreshes run every five seconds and estimates expire after ten active seconds. Shared requests are deduplicated briefly. The final local Chrome run measured **81, 20, 15 ms HTTP resource durations**. Earlier runs measured 5-58 ms. These are local fixture measurements, not deployment guarantees.
2. **Danger feedback:** both players receive the operator's Pokemon/scenario announcement, room name and action. A pulsing red edge leaves the center readable and controls clickable. Reduced-flash/reduced-motion modes use a static warning. Active emergencies retain their countdown; resolved alerts clear. All operator-injected incidents are announced globally; private partner puzzle evidence remains private.
3. **Fatal emergency response:** every confirmed emergency is blocking and expires exactly 60 active seconds after injection. The authoritative tick immediately fails the session if it remains unresolved. Pause freezes active time. Normal mission time continues during response and battle. Impossible 60-second placements are rejected during preview rather than quietly extending the limit. Operator cancellation remains distinct from successful response. Emergency terminals show ordered instructions for channels, delivery, partner activation or power proposal/approval as appropriate.
4. **Immediate prescription:** injection keys invalidate old recommendations on both player and operator views. Factual instructions identify the emergency, responder, location, input procedure and deadline consequence before numerical estimates return. The computed plan ranks four parallel/assigned/responder schedules by conditional success and successful completion time, then supplies an ordered A/B sequence. Existing active singles battles remain assigned to their actual owner. This is the best of the compared schedules, not a proof of the global optimum.
5. **Actual Pokemon battles:** `@pkmn/sim` **0.10.11**, the extracted Pokemon Showdown simulator, runs on the server only. Gen 9 custom singles use the trainer's three companions against one injected opponent, level 50, neutral nature, zero EVs and 31 IVs. One to four moves come from source learnsets, with actual PP, HP, accuracy, speed/priority, type effects, abilities, status, fainting and healthy switches. The opponent selects legal moves. Canonical IDs survive simulator party reordering. Cosmetic/unsupported form mappings are disclosed in the battle panel. Turn/request IDs reject stale choices; authoritative permissions, range, line of sight and cooldown checks remain. Old counter actions cannot bypass an active Showdown battle.
6. **First person:** the camera is at the player's head (height 46), with no shoulder offset or chase zoom. Own trainer geometry is hidden; the partner remains visible. Followers use a wider rear-side formation and fade only inside local camera clearance, including narrow passages. World collision/navigation remain canonical.
7. **Operator dashboard:** four live KPI cards, objective completion funnel, actual outcome donut, elapsed-time bars, injection response matrix, current response queue, predictive comparisons, prescriptive sequence and a cloned deadline sandbox replace the primary analytics presentation. Advanced records/tools remain available below. The style follows the supplied dark console/dashboard reference.

## Analytical meaning and limits

Live model: `ghost-shift-v10-route-workload-mc1`. This is a route/workload **timing Monte Carlo**, not a full simulator replay of future human puzzle inputs or battle turns. Travel follows current canonical paths at the gameplay speed; reading, work and move-choice durations are explicit uncertain assumptions. Blocking battle duration is an approximation, not a predicted exact damage sequence. Available party/legal equipment inputs are assumed. Optional encounters are bypassed by the compared mission schedules, with expiry/still-active outcomes reported; route comparisons separately expose linked guardian battle versus detour cost. Future operator injections are not predicted. Probabilities are conditional on these assumptions and are not calibrated visitor success rates. The separate offline reducer/path audit retains surrogate encounter counters and a different model ID (`ghost-shift-v10-reducer-audit-mc1`); its results are not relabeled as current live analysis.

The dashboard filters stored human sessions to the current scenario and reads up to 150 persisted sessions. Extraction rate uses finished rounds only; successful time excludes failed rounds. The funnel counts finished rounds reaching at least N of six tasks, not a fixed task order. Emergency and Pokemon outcomes, cancellations and first-response counts remain distinct. Empty means and percentages show an empty value instead of invented data. The response queue includes only active incidents in the current playing session. Generated estimates are separate from observed records.

## Battle presentation and source limits

Actual simulator move/hit/recovery/faint results drive the existing species-specific model/sprite renderer and typed effects. Misses and immunity do not generate fake damage. Native skeletal attack/idle clips play where present; static source models remain labeled static and use ambient/effect animation. This is an embedded singles battle interface, not the complete Showdown browser client, matchmaking service or every competitive format. HP/logs reflect the authoritative completed turn while timed model cues play. No new asset redistribution permission is implied; the existing non-commercial prototype source/rights notices and SpriteCollab credits remain applicable.
